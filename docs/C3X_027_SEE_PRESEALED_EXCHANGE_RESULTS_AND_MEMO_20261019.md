## Improved manuscript fragment: Material distinctions are easier than explanations
In nine previously selected chess positions, a binary response-coverage test identified only one root pair as different. We then froze a slightly richer outcome before rerunning the cases: the worst immediate material recovery against an opponent's capturing reply, including the first root's material gain. This test did not separate the earlier search-ranking development examples in positions 42 and 43. It did, however, reveal a difference in position 52 that the binary predicate had erased. Both root moves allowed a recapture after all eligible capturing replies; the local worst nominal material changes were +2 and 0. The difference is real under the stated two-response capture-only contract. It is not evidence that the chess engine used this material calculation, nor that either move is better against an unrestricted adversary. The result shows what precision can add to a proof-carrying explanation: a graded consequence may carry information that a yes/no tactical affordance discards. Whether this information is the right kind of information requires source-aligned computation and externally justified relevance.
