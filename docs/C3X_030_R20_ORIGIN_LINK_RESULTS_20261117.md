The [0.29 origin review](C3X_029_ORIGIN_RETURN_STRATEGIC_DIRECTION_REVIEW_20261031.md) asks why a move is marginally better and requires stable candidate selection and response-dependent consequences. In [C3X 32](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1pkMOuEEY5QEq5QhpYRnJ2Zx85LSmufz3/view) the user endorsed the [D1 choice](C3X_030_DECISION_D1_NATIVE_CAUSALITY_FIRST_20261111.md), which adds a stricter question about the actual calculations transmitting that preference and their link to future strategy. The endorsement followed a decision table that separately judges instrumentation, local effect, transmission, root choice, transport and strategic meaning.

R20 now has direct/native transmission to an early root update in one development case; a named-plan entry cost under independent positional evaluation; and a learner item with verified scoring. These measurements come from different positions. They cannot yet establish that the computation which favors a slightly better move is the computation that encodes the strategy a learner can use.

The next research cycle should use one new game-disjoint development queue and screen legal candidates for stable, near-equal preference at multiple budgets before reading event traces or choosing plan labels. Freeze outcome predicates and replies without strategic value labels, then compare three explanations built on the same candidate pair: ordinary PV, legal reply tree, and the same tree with native source evidence. A stable-pair source trace that changes intermediate scores but contributes no new correct reply prediction is a meaningful negative. Reserve a strategy or learner claim for the corresponding outcome in the same evidence regime. The held-out 0.29 and S4 cohorts stay sealed under their own contracts.
