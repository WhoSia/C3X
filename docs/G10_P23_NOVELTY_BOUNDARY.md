# C3X G10-P23 — Novelty Boundary against Causal Transport and Mechanistic Interpretability

## Purpose

This note is a pre-outcome novelty firewall. It records what P23 may and may not claim before seeing its prospective activation outcomes.

## Donor literature

### Transportability

Pearl & Bareinboim (2011), *Transportability of Causal and Statistical Relations: A Formal Approach*, formalize when causal/statistical relations learned in one environment can be transported to another and introduce selection diagrams.

Bareinboim & Pearl's limited-experiment transport work explicitly recognizes that some controlled experiments may not be feasible and asks which available experiments suffice for target causal identification.

Therefore C3X must **not** claim novelty for:
- the general idea that external causal validity requires assumptions about source/target differences;
- the general idea that experiment availability/feasibility matters;
- transportability, selection diagrams, or causal identification under limited experiments.

### Positivity / support

Positivity literature distinguishes structural impossibility from random empirical absence. It also makes clear that positivity alone is not a sufficient sample-size guarantee.

Therefore C3X must **not** equate its executable-support variable (R) with ordinary statistical positivity.

### Mechanistic interpretability

Causal abstraction work treats interventions and mechanism transformations as a formal basis for mechanistic interpretability.

Recent methodological critiques argue that mechanistic-interpretability causal claims need explicit identification assumptions and that validation metrics should not substitute for identification.

Therefore C3X must **not** claim novelty for causal intervention as an interpretability tool or for the general demand that causal claims state assumptions.

## P23's defensible incremental claim

C3X introduces a domain-specific operational support object for a contrastive mechanistic explanation experiment:

[
R(e,x)=1
]

iff the **already frozen** candidate pair and contrast grammar yield a deterministic, legal, repeatably measurable multi-arm intervention for engine (e) on world (x), before mediator-class response outcomes are opened.

The candidate contribution is the combination of four commitments:

1. **Operationalization** — intervention support is measured from executable engine/board operations rather than represented only as covariate overlap.
2. **Temporal separation** — (R) is sealed before activation response (A_f), preventing response-conditioned acquisition.
3. **Transport use** — a fresh validation sample is prospectively constructed under (R)-based support quotas before activation predictions/outcomes are adjudicated.
4. **Product authority use** — the identical support state becomes a machine-readable pre-causal firewall in the deployed explanation path; unsupported positions cannot surface mechanism-level causal language even when a certificate object is available.

The fourth point is a systems/authority contribution, not additional causal evidence.

Conflating any adjacent pair weakens the paper.

## Conditional estimand

P23 targets activation-field transport in the support-defined population:

[
P(A_f(e,x)=1mid R(e,x)=1,;X_{mathrm{BASE}}).
]

It does **not** identify population-wide activation over all chess worlds.

Conditioning on (R) may alter the target population. This is a limitation and should be stated as such, not hidden as preprocessing.

## Selection-bias question

The central design question is not whether selection occurs—P23 deliberately selects (R=1) units.

The question is whether selection is:
- prospectively defined;
- response-blind;
- based on the experimental intervention's realizability rather than its observed or predicted response;
- reported as defining the target population.

P23's cyclic source-balanced quota rule is intended to keep this distinction reconstructible.

## Mechanistic-interpretability relevance

P23 can be framed as an identification-discipline contribution:

> A mechanistic explanation claim should declare not only what intervention changes behavior, but also the domain on which the intervention is actually well-defined and reproducibly executable.

This aligns with recent demands for explicit causal-identification assumptions in mechanistic interpretability without claiming that C3X supplies a general identification theory.

## Reviewer-facing novelty sentence

Recommended narrow wording:

> We operationalize **executable intervention support** as a pre-response variable for a contrastive engine-mechanism experiment, prospectively use it to constitute a fresh validation population, and propagate the same support state into machine-readable causal-language abstention in the deployed explanation pipeline.

Avoid:
- “first causal transport method for mechanistic interpretability”;
- “new positivity theory”;
- “general solution to mechanistic interpretability identifiability”;
- “unbiased population-wide activation estimates”.
