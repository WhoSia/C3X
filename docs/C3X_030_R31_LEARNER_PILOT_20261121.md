## Administration and scoring

Give one lesson and its assessment from the assigned folder. Record chess
experience and the participant's initial candidate choice before the lesson.
Assign one arm per person and keep same-game and different-game item order and assessment timing
the same across arms. Close the lesson before administering the assessment.
Collect answers separately. The static practice pages submit no data.
Keep the existing R29 answer key with the organizer. Score without the arm
label if a human needs to adjudicate a response.

The primary pilot contrast is C−D for the joint per-item threat-and-plan
endpoint on four different-game positions, aggregated within participant.
Report the two same-game items separately. Retain both components, skipped
answers and a per-arm attempted-item count. Predefine how blank responses
are handled before seeing human results. B−A is a secondary lesson-package
contrast. Report reading time and perceived confidence separately from move
quality. Four cross-game variants of this one motif and three positive
vs one negative items are appropriate for checking the procedure. A
confirmatory test needs fresh hidden positions, a larger motif bank,
balanced positive and negative items and a power calculation for the
participant-level contrast.

C−D measures these two evidence panels as presented. Their different facts
are part of the treatment. The design controls common lesson wording,
panel length, formatting and known assessment items. Differences in
comprehensibility or perceived authority could still contribute to an
observed effect. There are no participants or observed human effects yet.

Cui, Ling and Ng establish the value of evaluating beyond the main line
in their strategy-verbalization task. Press and Golubev use engine-scored
positions for an automatic contrastive explanation metric. The latter
and the former inform different parts of this study. Neither serves as
a control group for it.
[Strategy verbalization](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-emnlp.1560/).
