## Chess planning and source attribution

- **Wilkins 1979, pp. 960–967, especially pp. 962–965.** The program represents a plan as a conditional expression with goals or moves for the offense and templates for the opponent's replies. THREAT, SAVE, LOSS and LIKELY are kept as separate attributes. At p. 965 a defense unmatched by the original template can restart the local search. A line can still win after the original plan stops applying. C3X transfer: future goal tests need an explicit predicate and a separate score for the intended plan's survival. [IJCAI proceedings, PDF pp. 351–358](https://drive.google.com/file/d/14s1jr9jb4_RaC59vLlxwylVzFG9_29gf/view#page=351).
- **Wilkins 1980, Artificial Intelligence 14(2), pp. 165–203.** Sections 2 and 5–9 detail production rules, plan structure, the granularity of plans and search control. In the worked comparison PARADISE produced 36 nodes and CAPS 489; Wilkins gives several explanations, including stronger knowledge, information returned by the tree and best-first behavior. The numbers do not isolate one component. The performance section distinguishes solving as is from solving after adding rules, recounts tuning on 21 developmental problems, and gives different time limits in the historical engine comparison. C3X transfer: hold the underlying reply tree, budgets and target items fixed when estimating the added contribution of an explanation. [Drive](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1GCMNqOUGX7FnF9vYQ5dNotEGS03NJNZ-/view).
- **Wilkins 1980, CSCSI, pp. 212–216, §§1–6.** Both Figures 1 and 2 permit the back-rank refutation after ...Rc5, but in Figure 2 ...Re5 attacks a possible interposition square and merits a search. PERMIT compares reasons why a move allows the refutation; AFFECT asks which features of an already searched line a new move changes. The tree stores move order and matched attacks, enabling richer comparisons than unordered sets. Section 6 states that the approach was effective and error-free in the author's 92-position domain and acknowledges that the influence rules can err in other middlegames. The paper establishes reason reuse and explicit countercausal checking as prior art. It does not intervene on a modern engine or test transfer to humans. [Five-page extraction](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1f1WsplBoN99OjD03kw6mqjQUZhvIKPzl/view) · [source volume](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1LiZJCgKmq87ot4-5yAkVGkwy8LUe__RX/view#page=221).

## What the explanation experiments measure

- **Hase & Bansal 2020, §§4.3–5, 7.** Forward simulation concerns an unseen input; counterfactual simulation supplies a perturbation of a known input. Their measured change compares matched tasks with and without explanations, balances data so merely copying the original prediction cannot work in the counterfactual task, and resamples people and items. Domains and methods differed, with clear benefits restricted to some settings. Participants' ratings did not predict the measured effectiveness. C3X transfer: write a forward outcome and a reply perturbation outcome, and balance answers so copying the original candidate rank is unhelpful. Separately score judgments on new chess positions. [Drive](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1GXj08LYZjYGKY6QiYlyo4JFnc0HBGQ0M/view).
- **Lai & Tan 2019, §§3–6 and Table 1.** In their deception task human-only control scored 51.1%, prediction-only without model accuracy 61.9%, explanation-only heatmap 57.6%, and label plus real heatmap 72.5%. Even a random heatmap or low-accuracy statement could affect trust in some settings. These are human task outcomes under study-specific conditions. C3X transfer: report suggestion, confidence and explanation as identifiable information inputs, and use an information-volume-matched control when measuring acceptance. [Drive](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1Sx4PE-HHuatot-rRwPhTAeqQBrLSsKG7/view).
- **Bansal et al. 2021, §§3–5, 7.** Used settings where AI and humans had comparable standalone performance so complementary decisions were feasible. Teams achieved complementary performance but explanations did not produce a significant accuracy increase over the confidence baseline. Agreement increased on both correct and incorrect AI cases. C3X transfer: score accepting a correct candidate and rejecting an incorrect suggested candidate separately. Add combined performance above both standalone baselines only for an assistive human task with the required baselines. [Drive](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1REWt7ZM5q4e6coHQxz_ylttG-ji9xa6G/view).
- **Doshi-Velez & Kim 2017, §§2–4.** Incompleteness motivates why a task metric can omit information relevant to interpretation. Their taxonomy distinguishes real tasks with appropriate people, simplified tasks with people, and proxy tasks that need no human evaluation. C3X engine-judged reply contrasts are proxy outcomes. Strategic transfer by learners needs a human task on new positions where task performance can be measured. [Drive](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1wUE1LRKvzRHmMobMbSNtsZPygNKmJ_E9/view).

## Local test and decision

R13 enumerates the legal responses already scored by the independent Berserk reference for each candidate and separates shared reply coordinates, branch-exclusive replies, and unrestricted minima. This checks how often a comparison tied to one opponent reply survives changing that reply. It does not treat coordinate identity as proof of an identical tactical role. A truly explanatory plan claim also needs a goal, a preserved consequence, and an explicit defeating reply, each bound to a legal history. The D1 incremental-information claim still needs a new same-tree trace/no-trace test. Only a matched trace effect on independent reply or goal prediction can justify that claim.
