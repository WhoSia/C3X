## 1. Primary prior art and what it does NOT entitle us to claim

- Knuth & Moore (1975), An Analysis of Alpha-Beta Pruning, *Artificial Intelligence* 6(4):293–326, DOI 10.1016/0004-3702(75)90019-3. The exact minimax/alpha-beta proof is not a direct theorem about selective SF16 with TT, aspiration, SEE, PVS and move ordering.
- Pearl (2001), Direct and Indirect Effects, UAI, doi:10.1145/3501714.3501736. Manipulating a TT consumer and observing a final move change identifies a treatment contrast in that experimental setting; it does not isolate a natural indirect effect.
- Avin, Shpitser & Pearl (2005), Identifiability of Path-Specific Effects, IJCAI pp.357–362. An event trace is not by itself a graphical path-specific identification proof. Causal paths must be defined and identifiability conditions met.
- Jacovi & Goldberg (2020), Towards Faithfully Interpretable NLP Systems, ACL pp.4198–4205, https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.386/. Human-plausible explanation and source-faithful mechanism description require separate evaluation.
