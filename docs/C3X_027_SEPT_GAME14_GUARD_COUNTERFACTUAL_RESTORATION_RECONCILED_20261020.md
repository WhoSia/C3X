## What is and is not proved

Under treated TT setting in this one selected game, bypassing the exact SEE-consuming capture guard changed the final root result back to original. This is an observed counterfactual causal effect of the guard bypass on the root in that intervention context. Not a natural or general TT→SEE→root mediation theorem, because the guard bypass is a second artificial intervention and selected after the root flip. Root choice restoration does not itself show chess strength or user learning.

## Revised manuscript Results

The intervention study initially identified one changed root move among the newly sampled games. The engine selected b3a4 without the transposition-table reader suppression and f3f4 with it. A source-chronology audit of the treated search then found one additional static-exchange test at a capture-pruning site, observed after the physical reader block. The test returned false and the original C++ guard skipped its candidate. A second, narrowly localized intervention held TT suppression fixed but reenabled exactly that guarded candidate. The final selected move returned to b3a4 in duplicate cold trials. This is a counterfactual link between a source guard and a concrete engine decision, although it remains a postselected mechanistic finding: the guard was chosen after the treatment's root-choice outcome was known. It establishes neither general natural mediation nor strategic superiority of either chess move.
