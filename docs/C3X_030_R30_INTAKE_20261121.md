Althöfer's chess account describes how a player can use changing
candidate moves, scores and principal variations displayed during
iterative deepening. One example is a line repeating moves despite
a substantially positive evaluation. The two-engine 3-Hirn gains
are historical reports with the author as the human decider, and
two other players reported difficulties replicating them. The Go
examples inspect the distribution of MCTS outcomes. They suggest
what an interface could expose. They do not establish the
causal effect of a Stockfish TT read or improved learner transfer.

The strongest cheap C3X comparison is therefore a public
iteration-history display (move, score/bound, PV) versus access
to the verified native intervention, holding the board explanation
fixed. Benchmark how often the public display distinguishes
effects on a score, an ordering and a final recommendation
before assuming the new source trace adds information for a person.

Kishimoto & Müller motivate retaining complete game histories and
avoiding the identification of transposed positions solely by FEN.
Their proof-number solution is separate from a Stockfish TT mask.
Hyatt & Cozzie study injected false hash matches. R30 keeps the match
and withholds one effective hint or value. Breuker et al. compare
table-retention policies. R30 preserves that policy and the table
size to localize effects at one observed read. See the
[R26 reading record](C3X_030_R26_TT_RESEARCH_AND_LITERATURE_20261117.md).
