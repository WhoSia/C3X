## Paper paragraph (new)
The earlier provenance problem was not that Stockfish's SEE invocation had never been observed. It was that the published summary omitted the individual source states. The original, pre-intervention target manifest still preserved two specific invocations: a qsearch-pruning SEE test of f6e4 at threshold -95 and a quiet-pruning test of d1e2 at threshold -43, with their narrow windows, depths and source-path signatures. We retrieved the source-only cohort and target manifest from their historical GitHub Actions runs and independently verified their hashes and corresponding legal root positions. That recovery is enough to specify an exact replay experiment. It is not enough to infer what exchanges the SEE routine evaluated at the moment of invocation. The original root board and an internal recursive-search board can contain different pieces even when the encoded move is legal in both. The next scientific observation must therefore be a passive reconstruction of the source-matched internal board at the SEE call. Only then can a chess-legal exchange claim be compared to the measured operator's threshold decision.
