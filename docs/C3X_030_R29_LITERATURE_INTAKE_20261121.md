| Reading | Exact scope and decision |
| --- | --- |
| [Schemmer et al. (2023), arXiv v3](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1mhYV9VNVAxYugzyEzB7BTC0q-QMn6OAN/view) | Sections 3, 5 and 6 and Table 3. The advice came after an initial decision. Adoption of correct advice on initially wrong decisions was 29.59% in control and 38.87% with feature importance. Retention of a correct initial judgment when AI advice was wrong was 71.87% and 69.45%, with no significant difference. Keep the two conditional denominators in R28. The task was deception detection. Its effect sizes do not enter a chess power calculation. |
| [Wang & Yin (2021), conference paper](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1T90kjp2TwjLU8qu1NRHLdLgOvSXeoYQS/view) | Sections 3–5 and Table 2. Four explanation types performed differently for recidivism and forest-cover decisions. Feature contributions supported overall trust calibration on the former task among the compared methods. Domain knowledge was itself task-dependent, not a separately randomized factor. Measure chess experience in C3X and prespecify descriptive subgroup analysis. |
| [Poursabzi-Sangdeh et al. (2021), arXiv v5](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1B96x_E3rrkhD0W6P4sMsYQ3C7mAk2m9_/view) | Main experiments and Sections 7–8. Functionally fixed apartment-price models changed in feature count and transparency. Participants could simulate a clear small model more accurately. Simulability brought no significant increase in use of the advice, and transparency could impede correction of large errors. C3X treats prediction, error correction and transfer as separate outcomes. An information-load mechanism in C3X remains a hypothesis. |
| [Renkl et al. (2002), author manuscript](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1vRz3lVZ9bCMX0y59GbN9sylISlrHFdBH/view) | Field experiment on electricity and lab experiment on probability. The field ANCOVA supported the near-transfer effect; the far-transfer difference was inconclusive. Sections on task construction, especially whether the same principle is required, matter here. Use both changed replies in the same game and other games. The latter still reuse a specific chess motif and are not evidence of far transfer across strategies. |
| [Atkinson, Renkl & Merrill (2003), journal text with a repository cover](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1t_vqezZnWquPljkPUg96z2Sw_l1y6WWw/view) | Two probability experiments, including the classroom evaluation. Fading plus prompts about the governing principle supported near and farther problem transfer in their comparisons. Where the learner sees fading, give every C3X arm the same sequence and same prompts. Otherwise access to the source trace would be confounded with active practice. |
| [Jacobs et al. (2021), publisher PDF](https://drive.google.com/file/d/16UlRDWjT8FQkcFAjWtO36hDmw2Dwsyrg/view) | Methods and Tables 2–3. Clinicians chose treatments for written vignettes. The simulated wrong-advice condition was weaker than baseline on guideline-referenced choices; explanations varied and the feature-based wrong-advice condition had the lowest mean in Table 3. The relevant intervention here is to include corroborated weak chess advice and measure concrete choices, rather than equating the user's confidence with improved judgment. |
