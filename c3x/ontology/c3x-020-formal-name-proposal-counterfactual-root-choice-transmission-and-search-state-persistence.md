The last bounded source experiment [#38031332649](https://github.com/WhoSia/C3X/actions/runs/38031332649), on four February roots selected after the independent F19.5 failure, surgically restored each **first observed semantically different root child return** to its F-world value, after the child search/undo and before the root score update. Native source contact, score restore and sham controls succeeded. The scientific prediction that a single such site should restore at least one final original bestmove **K4 FAILED (0/4)**. In game6, +114→+103 removed a beta crossing and restored depth10 root-trial exit to 103, but final bestmove still did not return to its F choice. In games3/7/10, edited returns remained under local alpha and the entire six-field final UCI matched the unrescued arm.

Thus a physically real intervention and a perfectly matched **local** root score do not necessarily restore the later decision. The next object is **search-state persistence between the edited return, root trial, and later iterative deepening**, not a repeatedly refined writer-epoch tag.

## Formal research primitive (scope-limited)

Let `H_d` denote the observed (not necessarily complete) state after a depth-`d` root trial: root move scores, average scores, PV/order, aspiration history, current TT contents, search statistics, and remaining search heuristics. Let `C_d` denote current child return and `R_{d+1}` the subsequent root decision state. The observed non-identification is

`do(C_d := C_d^F)` with `H_d` otherwise previously exposed to V intervention **does not in general imply** `R_final = R_final^F`.

This is an empirical counterexample on four fixed source games, not a universal theorem about chess. It motivates *testing* a competitor state history hypothesis, not assuming one.
